# Write Simply

Inspired by Paul Graham's [Write Simply](https://www.paulgraham.com/simply.html).

The goal: readers should find the ideas in their heads and barely notice the words. Writing is just
a delivery mechanism. Make it disappear.

---

## Principles

Load this skill when the work is mostly writing. For ordinary coding, debugging, or tool work, use it
only when the user asks for a prose deliverable or a simpler explanation.

### 1. Use ordinary words

If a simpler word exists, use it. Not "utilize" — "use". Not "commence" — "start". Not "endeavour"
— "try".

Would you say this word out loud in conversation? If not, reconsider it.

### 2. Write short sentences

Long sentences make readers hold too much at once. When a sentence runs long, split it. One idea
per sentence is a good default.

### 3. Cut

Most first drafts are too long. Cut:

- **Throat-clearing** — openers that warm up but say nothing
- **Hedges** — "it could be argued that", "in some sense"
- **Redundancy** — saying the same thing twice
- **Filler** — "it is important to note that", "needless to say"
- **Weak intensifiers** — "very", "really", "quite", "rather"

Test each sentence: is it doing work? If not, delete it.

### 4. Be concrete

Abstract writing is hard to follow. Use specific examples, numbers, or images.

Instead of: *"There are significant advantages to this approach."*
Write: *"This saves three steps and halves the error rate."*

### 5. Write honestly

If you have nothing to say, simple writing makes that obvious — to you and to the reader. Fancy
writing hides empty thinking. Simple writing can't.

If what you want to say is fuzzy, think first. Write second.

### 6. Write for non-native speakers

Many readers understand ideas faster than they understand English. A hard topic doesn't earn hard
words. A hard idea in plain words is clearer — and often more impressive.

### 7. Use plain structure

- **Short paragraphs** — white space helps
- **Active voice** — "the team decided", not "a decision was made"
- **Lead with the point** — don't bury it

---

## Process

Write the first draft fast. Then edit. Most editing is cutting.

1. **Draft without stopping.** Get the ideas down.
2. **Read it aloud.** Your ear catches what your eye misses.
3. **Cut the first paragraph.** It's usually warm-up. See if the piece works without it.
4. **Go sentence by sentence.** Is this doing work? Is there a simpler version?
5. **Read it as a stranger.** Does it make sense? Is anything unclear?

## Words to Replace

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| "It is worth noting that..." | Just say it |
| "In order to" | "To" |
| "Due to the fact that" | "Because" |
| "At this point in time" | "Now" |
| "Facilitate" | "Help" |
| "Leverage" / "Utilize" | "Use" |
| "Going forward" | Cut it |
| "Impactful" | Say what the impact is |
| "Mistakes were made" | "We made mistakes" |
| "Make a decision" | "Decide" |

---

## When Complexity Is Fine

Some ideas need long sentences or unusual words. That's fine — if it's intentional. The rule:
complexity in service of the idea, not in service of sounding smart.

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Can any word be replaced with a shorter one?
- [ ] Is every sentence doing work?
- [ ] Can any sentence be split?
- [ ] Did I cut the throat-clearing at the start?
- [ ] Is the main point near the top?
- [ ] Would a non-native speaker follow this?
- [ ] Does it sound like something a person would say?

## Before / After

**Before:**
> It is important to note that, due to the fact that the implementation of the proposed solution
> will require significant resource allocation on the part of the engineering team, it may be
> necessary to consider the possibility of extending the timeline in order to ensure that quality
> standards are adequately maintained.

**After:**
> This will take more engineering time than planned. We should extend the deadline to keep quality up.

Same information. One-fifth the words.
